# Rating songs with evidence

Scores run from **0 to 10, including 10**. Ten means exemplary on a specified
criterion for the evidence reviewed, not a claim that a song is perfect in every
respect. A 2 or 3 means major, demonstrated weaknesses obstruct that criterion;
it should have a concrete explanation. Missing evidence is **unassessed**, never
zero. Whole-number judgments are usually easier to defend than fine decimals.

| Score | Evidence anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0–1 | Fundamental failure: almost entirely fails the stated criterion. |
| 2–3 | Several major problems substantially obstruct its purpose. |
| 4–5 | Mixed strengths and recurring weaknesses requiring explanation or adaptation. |
| 6–7 | Substantially meets the criterion with identifiable limitations. |
| 8–9 | Very strong, with only minor specific limitations. |
| 10 | Fully meets the criterion; no material weakness found on the evidence reviewed. |

The rating profile contains the same anchors and an editable evidence rubric for
each criterion. Criteria can be deactivated, removed, restored or added. Scores
are retained when a criterion is removed. Restoring a built-in criterion uses its
original ID; a newly created custom criterion has its own ID. For a temporary
change, deactivate a custom criterion so it can be reactivated with its scores.

An average is the sum of **score × weight**, divided by the weights of active,
assessed criteria. Missing values and inactive/zero-weight criteria are excluded.
“Use equal weights” makes this the ordinary average of the selected scores.
Existing weights are preserved until you change them; no old reviews are silently
regraded. Serious doctrinal concerns and human church-use decisions remain
separate from the average.

## Criterion rules

| Criterion | What to assess | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doctrinal accuracy | Compare explicit claims with the church's stated convictions and Scripture in context. Explain a disputed interpretation. | Penalizing a song for omitting doctrines outside its subject; judging its artist by association. |
| Theological substance | Look for specific, coherent development of the song's actual subject, relationships and imagery. | Equating long texts or technical vocabulary with depth. |
| Language & craftsmanship | Check complete thoughts, referents, intelligible images, progression and repetition's function. | Treating poetry, dialect or simple vocabulary as inherently inferior. |
| Arrangement coherence | Examine the actual version's progression, repeats, pauses, wordless and instrumental passages against a stated purpose. | “Shorter is better”; inferring structure or duration from a title. |
| Congregational singability | Examine the chosen melody and key: range, tessitura, leaps, phrase length and rhythmic learnability for this congregation. | Inferring difficulty from lyrics or genre alone. |
| Corporate participation | Assess meaningful participation and shared expression, teachability and the actual version used. | Assuming “I” is inferior to “we”; a singular confession can be shared. |
| Emotional and pastoral honesty | Assess truthful expression of joy, grief, doubt, repentance and hope in context. | Rewarding emotional intensity or treating lament as inferior to celebration. |
| Scriptural faithfulness | Verify identifiable quotations and allusions, with context and sources. | Counting references as a quality score or penalizing songs that are not paraphrases. |

The final four are optional suggested criteria. Accuracy and scriptural
faithfulness overlap, as do substance and craftsmanship; avoid double-counting
unless you define distinct questions. Musical ratings require arrangement evidence.
The old “Purposeful use of time” field retains its stored scores under the new
“Arrangement coherence” label; old judgments may deserve a human rereview.

To make judgments more consistent, identify the exact text/recording, specify the
criterion and congregation, cite the observable feature, apply the same anchor,
and name uncertainty. Where reviewers disagree, compare their evidence and
rubrics before averaging their numbers. Theological interpretation and pastoral
suitability involve human judgment; these rules improve consistency rather than
making them mechanically objective.

## Library balance is a separate question

A library audit describes direction (to/about God, to the church, testimony),
named Father/Son/Spirit references, corporate/individual voice, lament and
celebration, textual function and recorded tempo bands. These are distribution
questions, not universal quality scores. No library must contain equal thirds of
Trinitarian references or equal numbers of lament and celebration songs. State
the congregation's purpose before deciding whether a distribution is a weakness.

Run Library Audit prepares a ChatGPT request and imports a JSON response into a
temporary visual report. Counts and percentages are calculated locally, including
unknowns and evidence coverage. Trinity references may overlap; tempo uses only
recorded BPM, which does not establish perceived energy. Sources and conclusions
still require verification. Report download retains a snapshot; the report never
changes scores, approvals, libraries or service plans. Private notes and plans are
excluded; including stored song words requires explicit opt-in.
